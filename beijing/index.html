    About the Moon

    What is the Moon's phase today?

    The Moon's phase is how much of its sunlit half we can see from Earth. Moonwatch calculates it live for the selected city, shows the exact percentage illuminated, and turns the 3D Moon to match what you see in your own sky. A full cycle from new moon to new moon takes about 29.5 days.

    When is the next full moon?

    A full moon comes about every 29.5 days, when the Moon is opposite the Sun in the sky. The countdown near the top of this page shows the exact date and local time of the next full moon, and the phase calendar lists the coming new moon, first quarter, full moon and last quarter.

    What are the eight phases of the Moon?

    New moon, waxing crescent, first quarter, waxing gibbous, full moon, waning gibbous, last quarter and waning crescent. Waxing means the lit part grows each night; waning means it shrinks.

    Why does the Moon rise at a different time every day?

    The Moon moves about 13° along its orbit each day, so the Earth has to turn a little further before the Moon comes up again. On average moonrise is about 50 minutes later each day, but it varies a lot with the season and with how far north or south you are.

    Why does the Moon look upside down in the southern hemisphere?

    People south of the equator see the Moon from the other side of the Earth, so it appears turned by about 180 degrees: a waxing Moon is lit on the left instead of the right. Moonwatch shows the Moon tilted exactly as it looks from the city you choose.

    Can I see the Moon in daylight?

    Yes. The Moon is above the horizon for about half of every day, and for much of the month part of that time falls in daylight. It is easiest to spot as a pale disc in the afternoon around first quarter and in the morning around last quarter.
